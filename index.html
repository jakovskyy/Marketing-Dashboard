The third workflow. Maintenance keeps published content correct as the world moves: tax thresholds change, rates move, a deferred feature goes live. It is ongoing and triggered by facts changing, not by a positioning decision.

Status: Design reserved. Automation deferred until the pipeline has run real cycles.

● The trigger — the part maintenance doesn't share with the pipeline

The rewrite half reuses the pipeline. The real design work is the signal that answers "which existing article needs attention, and why." Three ways to generate it:

1
Fact volatility Primary · buildable now
A number or rule inside the article is now wrong. Highest value for numbers-heavy content. Runs off the volatile-facts register. No integrations needed.
2
Performance decay Blocked
Traffic or rankings dropped. Strong signal, but needs GA4 and SEMrush position tracking connected. This is the main thing between us and full automation.
3
Time decay Fallback only
Anything past a set age gets a look. Crude and mostly wasteful. Use as a periodic safety net, not the main driver.

● Volatile-facts register

Every time-sensitive fact mapped to the articles that cite it. When a fact changes, every article that depends on it is flagged. Values stay unverified until a verification pass confirms them, so a wrong figure never enters the backbone.

● Cadence

  • Event-driven. A tracked fact changes, affected articles flag immediately. Scheduled changes like the FHFA loan limit each November can be calendared.
  • Quarterly sweep. Every article checked against the register, and against performance data once it's connected.
  • Annual deep pass. A fuller review, closer to a re-audit.

● Blocked on

  • The performance-decay trigger waits on GA4 and SEMrush position tracking.

● The RAG agent

The agent has not run yet. It runs with the quarterly facts sweep, and on demand the moment a new article ships.

This agentic workflow takes on two paths. Every chunk edit and Notion correction stays with Jacob, as it is with the pipeline; and in each case it reports, judges, and proposes material.

1
Drift 18 articles · 51 chunks
It compares each source article's current version in Notion against the copy it saved earlier. When an article has changed, it reports which chunks come from that article and whether the change actually affects each one. The Buyers Guide, Understanding Title Insurance, and What Is a Title Commitment together supply 26 of the 51 chunks, so any edit to those three goes straight to review.
2
Intake 22 articles waiting
It looks at articles that are new to the corpus, whether newly audited, still unaudited, or sitting in Drafts. It reports whether each one should become a chunk in an existing pillar, or whether it belongs with other articles around a subject that no pillar covers yet. Thirteen audited articles and nine unaudited or draft articles do not reach Tilo at all, and that is the larger opportunity.
Chunks awaiting review: 0 Intake without a disposition: 0
It runs alongside the quarterly facts sweep on the first of January, April, July, and October, so one sweep produces two reports. It also runs on demand. You do not have to wait for the sweep. When a new article ships from the pipeline, the intake check can run right then, and you can point the agent at a single article at any time. On its first run it saves a copy of each of the 18 source articles. After that, every run compares the current article against the saved copy to see exactly what changed. When several articles cluster around a subject that no pillar covers, the agent flags the pattern, but it does not create the pillar, because naming a pillar is a decision for you.

● How it connects to the other three

  • Audit → RAG. Chunks sit downstream of audited articles. When an article is corrected, every chunk citing it has to move with it. 44 of the 57 chunks trace to audited sources.
  • Maintenance → RAG. The volatile-facts register maps time-sensitive facts to the articles that cite them. 51 chunks carry a review_by date set by volatility, not the calendar, and the quarterly facts sweep should eventually reach them.
  • Pipeline → RAG. New articles are candidates for new chunks. Nothing links the two today, which is part of why 13 audited articles still feed nothing.

● What Closing Map is

A consumer-facing real estate service. It educates buyers and sellers and gives them a transparent path from offer to close. They run calculations, understand their numbers, build a profile, prepare documents, and assemble their team. By the time they talk to a professional, they have already done the work. Everyone starts ahead.

● What it is not (hard lines)

  • A listings marketplace. We do not compete with Zillow, StreetEasy, or the MLS.
  • A CRM. That is CloseProp.
  • A lead generation tool. We do not sell consumer data to professionals.
  • A transaction management platform. That is also CloseProp.

● The three products — do not conflate

Closing Map
Consumer-facing. Education, calculators, profiles, team assembly, readiness scoring. Pre-transaction through closing.
CloseProp
Transaction coordination. The shared workspace for consumers and their team once a property is under contract.
Tilo (TytlLab)

● Tone & editorial standards

  • Declarative and direct. Make claims. Do not hedge.
  • Conversational and formal. Educated peer, not salesperson. The consumer is not a moron.
  • Benefit in the headline. The reader should not have to complete a thought.
  • Facts over adjectives. A specific dollar amount beats any superlative.
  • One big idea per piece. Prepared buyers and sellers make better decisions.
  • CTAs are promises, not instructions.
  • No em dashes. No oppositional language. Restructure the sentence instead.
